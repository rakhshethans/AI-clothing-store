# Interview and usability pack

Recruit 12–15 UK adult students with a recent real online clothing purchase/recreation attempt.
Start with six, at least some beyond friends. Ask permission to take notes; no recording by default.
Use participant codes, not unnecessary personal data. Do not represent interest as product usage.

Interview, 15 minutes:
1. Show me the last look you wanted to recreate. What actually happened?
2. Which tools/sites did you use, and where did you stop?
3. Was size, delivered price, style, quality or trust the main obstacle?
4. What did you buy? What did you spend? What did you already own?
5. Try this on a current task. Which results would you actually consider and why?
6. When will you next need this? May we invite you for a second task? Keep consent separately.

Observed prototype tasks, 10 minutes:
- Find a jacket below £65; narrow by colour or shop; explain what stock/size labels mean.
- Open a product and save it. Reload and find it again.
- Build a three-piece outfit below £120 including delivery. Mark one garment owned.
- Try a too-low budget. Does the failure message make sense?
- Add/remove a reference photo. Explain what the app did, and did not, infer from it.
- Inspect a sample retailer link. Can you tell that it is not a real purchasing integration?

Because inventory is synthetic, this test can validate usability, not demand/conversion or real
matching quality. Follow with a separate permissioned concierge test on actual shopping requests.
Benchmark 10 identical tasks with the participant's existing tools. Record time, acceptable items,
known shipping and reasons for rejection. Do not optimise only on favourable examples.

Creator pilot: ask 3–5 creators how many item-link requests they get, how they currently tag items,
whether preparing a page saves effort, and whether they actually share and repeat a post. No income
promise. Obtain photographer/subject/product image rights before publishing any real page.
